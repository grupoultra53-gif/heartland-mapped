How did the biggest ship on the Great Lakes vanish in minutes without ever sending an SOS?

Wait... let's rewind.

Launched in 1958, the 729-foot SS Edmund Fitzgerald was the pride of the American side.

On November 9, 1975, she left Superior, Wisconsin, loaded with 26,000 tons of iron ore bound for Detroit.

The next day, a ferocious November gale slammed Lake Superior with hurricane-force winds and thirty-foot waves.

Her radar failed, deck fittings snapped, and the giant freighter began listing heavily in blinding snow.

At 7:10 p.m., Captain Ernest McSorley radioed a nearby ship with his final words: "We are holding our own."

Minutes later, just seventeen miles from Whitefish Bay, the Mighty Fitz vanished completely from radar.

All twenty-nine crewmen were lost, and the massive hull was found snapped in two 530 feet below.

Whether rogue waves, flooded hatches, or a hidden shoal doomed her remains Lake Superior's greatest mystery.

Every November 10th, the museum bell rings thirty times—for the twenty-nine sailors and singer Gordon Lightfoot.
