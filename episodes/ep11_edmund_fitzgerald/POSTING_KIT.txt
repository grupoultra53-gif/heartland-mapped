How did the biggest ship on the Great Lakes vanish in minutes without ever sending an SOS? Launched in 1958, the 729-foot SS Edmund Fitzgerald was the pride of the American side. On November 10, 1975, a ferocious November gale slammed Lake Superior with hurricane-force winds and 30-foot waves.

Her radar failed and deck fittings snapped in blinding snow. At 7:10 p.m., Captain Ernest McSorley radioed his final words: "We are holding our own." Minutes later—just 17 miles from the shelter of Whitefish Bay—she vanished from radar, found snapped in two 530 feet below. Every November 10th, the museum bell rings 30 times for the 29 crewmen and singer Gordon Lightfoot.
