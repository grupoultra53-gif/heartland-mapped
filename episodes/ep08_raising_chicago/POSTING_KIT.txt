3. YOUTUBE SHORTS — DESCRICAO (COPIAR E COLAR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
How did Chicago physically jack up an entire city ten feet out of the mud without closing a single hotel? In the 1850s, Chicago sat on flat marshy wetlands barely higher than Lake Michigan. After a deadly 1854 cholera epidemic, engineer Ellis Chesbrough proposed laying brick sewers above ground, burying them, and lifting entire city blocks up on iron jackscrews.

At a foreman's whistle blast, hundreds of laborers turned every screw a tiny quarter-turn in unison. In 1861, 500 men used 1,000 jackscrews to raise the six-story Tremont House hotel six feet while guests slept, dined, and walked out the lobby completely undisturbed—and wooden shops even rolled down the streets on logs!
