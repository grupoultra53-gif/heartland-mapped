In 1839, Iowa and Missouri called up thousands of armed militiamen and nearly went to war... over three honey trees.

Wait—let's rewind.

In 1816, surveyors drew Missouri's northern border straight east from the rapids of the Des Moines River.

Twenty years later, Missouri claimed they'd used the wrong rapids—and pushed the border nine miles north, claiming 2,600 square miles of Iowa farmland.

When Missouri sent a sheriff to collect taxes, Iowa's governor—the very same fiery leader from Ohio's Toledo War—had him arrested on the spot.

Then came the breaking point: a Missouri farmer snuck across the line and chopped down three hollow bee trees to steal the honey.

Furious Iowans fined him a dollar fifty, newspapers dubbed it the Honey War, and both governors ordered their militias to march.

Over a thousand Iowans marched south in freezing December armed with rusty muskets, pitchforks, frying pans—and even a sausage stuffer.

Shivering in the bitter cold, both sides realized nobody wanted to die over honey—and marched right back home without firing a single shot.

Ten years later, the U.S. Supreme Court unanimously sided with Iowa, restoring the original 1816 border with permanent iron posts.

Not one drop of blood was ever spilled—making the Honey War the sweetest standoff in American history.

Zero casualties, three bee trees lost. So be honest... if your neighbor stole your honey trees, would you call out the militia too?
