How did one spring flood trap an entire Iowa town inside Nebraska overnight?

Wait... let's rewind.

In 1846, the Missouri River formed the clean border between Iowa and Nebraska—looping into a giant horseshoe bend just north of Omaha.

Then in March 1877, a massive ice jam and spring flood slammed the river.

Overnight, raging floodwaters burst straight across the narrow neck, carving a brand-new river channel over a mile east.

Left behind was a quiet horseshoe lake—and 1,300 acres of Iowa suddenly stranded on Nebraska's side of the river.

Nebraska wasted zero time: "It's on our side now—hand it over!" Iowa fired back: "Not a chance."

In 1892, the U.S. Supreme Court stepped in with an ancient rule: slow river shifts move a border, but a sudden flood jump does not.

The border stayed frozen in the old lakebed—handing a unanimous victory to Iowa.

Today, nearly 3,800 folks live in Carter Lake—the only Iowa town west of the Missouri River. Even drivers leaving Omaha's airport cut straight through Iowa!

To drive to the rest of Iowa, locals literally have to cross into Nebraska first.

Comment IOWA to keep it, or NEBRASKA to annex it—and now you know...
