3. YOUTUBE SHORTS — DESCRICAO (COPIAR E COLAR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
How did one five-mile bridge end twenty-mile traffic jams that split Michigan in half? For decades, reaching Michigan's Upper Peninsula meant lining up for state auto ferries across the Straits of Mackinac—turning summer vacations and deer hunting season into all-day waits, while winter ice jams stranded the Upper Peninsula completely.

Engineer David Steinman answered critics with an open-grate roadway built to let fierce winds pass straight through. Thousands of ironworkers spun 42,000 miles of steel cable, and on November 1, 1957, the five-mile Mackinac Bridge opened to cheers and honking horns—turning hours of dock waiting into a ten-minute drive above the Great Lakes. Over 200 million vehicles have crossed Big Mac since!
