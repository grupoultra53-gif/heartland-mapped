3. YOUTUBE SHORTS — DESCRICAO (COPIAR E COLAR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
How did one sunny Sunday afternoon turn pitch black in minutes across six American states? Years of deep plowing had ripped away the native prairie grass that held the Great Plains together, and when multi-year drought struck, millions of acres turned to fine powder.

On Sunday morning, April 14, 1935, families across Kansas, Oklahoma, and Nebraska enjoyed bright sunshine—until a towering black wall of dust raced south at 60 miles an hour. Midday turned darker than midnight, static electricity shorted out cars, and families scrambled indoors to stuff wet sheets under every crack. The storm inspired reporter Robert Geiger to coin the phrase "the Dust Bowl," inspired Woody Guthrie's songs, and reshaped American farming forever with contour plowing and windbreaks.
