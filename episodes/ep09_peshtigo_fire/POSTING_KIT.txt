How did America's deadliest wildfire kill five times more people than Chicago on the exact same night—and get forgotten? Everyone remembers October 8, 1871, as the night the Great Chicago Fire claimed 300 lives. Yet 250 miles north in Wisconsin, a roaring 2,000-degree firestorm swept through the lumber town of Peshtigo, burning over one million acres across Wisconsin and Michigan.

Terrified families stood neck-deep in the freezing Peshtigo River to survive as embers rained from the sky. Between 1,200 and 2,500 people perished—the deadliest wildfire in recorded history—yet because the flames melted Peshtigo's telegraph wires immediately, the outside world stayed silent for days.
