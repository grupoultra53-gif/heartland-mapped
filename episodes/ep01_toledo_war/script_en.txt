In 1835, Ohio and Michigan sent armed militias to the border over a narrow strip of swamp... and the loser actually walked away with the better deal.

Wait—let's rewind.

In 1787, lawmakers drew the border straight east from the southern tip of Lake Michigan.

The problem? Their old map was completely wrong.

When real surveyors arrived, both sides claimed the Toledo Strip—a vital shipping port on Lake Erie.

Ohio's governor refused to back down. Neither did Michigan's fiery 23-year-old governor.

Soon, armed militias were marching straight toward each other.

They called it the Toledo War... though it was mostly shouting and warning shots. The only blood spilled? A Michigan deputy stabbed with a pocketknife in a tavern brawl.

With an election coming, President Andrew Jackson needed Ohio's votes. So Congress handed Toledo to Ohio...

...and gave Michigan a consolation prize: the frozen Upper Peninsula.

Michigan felt robbed... until massive copper and iron deposits were discovered up north, powering American industry for decades.

Today, that old border grudge still lives on every November on the football field. So be honest... who really won the trade? Ohio or Michigan?
